# Customer Journey Map: SmartProductDiscoveryAI System

## Persona: The Frustrated Product Manager

**Name:** Sarah Chen
**Title:** Senior Product Manager, B2B SaaS Company (500 employees)
**Experience:** 6 years in product management
**Team:** 4 product managers, 2 designers, 12 engineers

### Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)

- **Primary JTBD:** "Help me discover what to build next based on real evidence instead of guesswork, so I can deliver features that actually move metrics."
- **Secondary JTBD:** "Help me synthesize scattered customer feedback, support tickets, and analytics data into a coherent discovery narrative I can share with leadership."
- **Emotional JTBD:** "Make me feel confident and in control of the discovery process instead of overwhelmed and behind."

### Current State Before Discovery

Sarah spends 10+ hours per week manually cobbling together insights from Google Sheets, Intercom, Mixpanel, Slack channels, and Jira. She pastes quotes into spreadsheets, tags them by hand, and tries to find patterns. Her roadmap is driven by the loudest stakeholder, not the strongest signal. She knows there has to be a better way.

## Stage 4: Activation (The "Aha" Moment)

**"Wait... it found something I would have missed."**

### Scenario

Sarah's first AI discovery cycle completes. Among the generated insights, one stands out: a recurring theme among churned enterprise customers that was buried across 47 different Intercom conversations and 12 support tickets. The AI surfaced a specific feature gap that her manual process had completely missed. She immediately screenshots it and sends it to her lead engineer with the message: "Look at this."

### Detail Breakdown

| Dimension | Details |
|---|---|
| **Touchpoints** | Discovery Brief output screen, insight detail view, evidence trail (linked source conversations), share/export functionality, in-app notification ("New insight discovered"), email summary of discovery results |
| **User Actions** | Reads the Discovery Brief; clicks into a specific insight; reviews the supporting evidence (linked Intercom conversations, support tickets); verifies the insight against her own knowledge; screenshots and shares with a colleague; exports the brief as a PDF; runs a second discovery cycle with refined parameters |
| **Thoughts & Questions** | "Wow, I spent all weekend on my spreadsheet and didn't find this." / "Can I trust this?" (checks the evidence links) / "This would have taken me days to find manually." / "My team needs to see this." / "What else can this find?" / "I need to present this at our next roadmap review." |
| **Emotions** | Surprised and delighted (5/5), validated (4.5/5), excited about possibilities (5/5), slightly skeptical but impressed (4/5) |
| **Pain Points** | Insight confidence scoring is missing -- she cannot tell how strong the signal is; evidence links are hard to navigate (too many clicks); cannot easily annotate or add context to an insight; sharing is limited to screenshots (no interactive sharing for non-users); no way to mark an insight as "actionable" vs. "interesting but not now" |
| **Opportunities** | Add a confidence score and signal strength indicator to each insight; create an "Insight Card" view that shows the finding + top 3 evidence snippets in one glance; enable one-click sharing via a unique link (recipients can view without signing up); add a "Save to Roadmap" or "Create Jira Ticket" action directly from the insight; include a "Refine This Insight" prompt for follow-up; show a before/after comparison: "Manual process: 10 hours. AI Discovery: 15 minutes." |

### This is the Critical "Aha Moment"

The activation event is when the PM sees an insight that is **(a)** non-obvious, **(b)** directly tied to a business metric they care about, and **(c)** backed by traceable evidence from their own data. If this moment does not happen in the first session, the trial is at severe risk of abandonment.

## Critical Moments

### Aha Moment (Activation)

**When:** The PM sees their first non-obvious, evidence-backed insight from their own data that they would have missed manually.

**Trigger:** The AI surfaces a pattern buried across dozens of scattered data sources (Intercom conversations, support tickets, NPS responses) and presents it as a clear, actionable finding with linked evidence.

**Metric to track:** Percentage of trial users who view an insight detail page with evidence links within the first session.

### Moments of Truth

| Moment | Stage | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Seeing the demo video | Consideration | "Does this look real or staged?" |
| First data source connection | Onboarding | "Will this work with my data?" |
| First insight review | Activation | "Is this genuinely useful or just AI fluff?" |
| First sharing with a colleague | Activation | "Will my team take this seriously?" |
| Team rollout conversation | Expansion | "Is this worth fighting for internally?" |
| Renewal decision | Expansion | "Did this actually make us better at product?" |

### Churn Triggers

| Trigger | Stage | Risk Level | Root Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| First discovery cycle produces generic insights | Onboarding / Activation | Critical | AI did not find anything surprising or actionable |
| Data source connection fails or requires IT | Onboarding | High | Friction before value delivery |
| AI produces a "hallucination" or wrong insight | Regular Use | High | Trust erosion; PM cannot rely on the output |
| Team members do not adopt the tool | Expansion | Medium | Social proof fails; tool becomes single-player |
| Cannot demonstrate ROI | Expansion / Renewal | High | No clear connection between insights and business outcomes |
| Competitor offers similar feature at lower cost | Consideration / Renewal | Medium | Lack of differentiation and switching cost |
